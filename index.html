Terrain and earthworks

Site Grading

Finds the platform level and slope that fits your plot and balances cut against fill, so less soil leaves or enters the site. Elevation data is fetched automatically from the national elevation model.

You provide

  • Platform area as a polygon
  • Design constraints such as maximum slope

You get

  • Optimised platform level and fall
  • Cut and fill volumes and mass balance
  • Cut and fill slopes outside the platform, with daylighting to existing ground
  • Maps and a report, with terrain as raster and shapefile

Why it matters: on sloping or rocky plots, earthworks can add real cost and decide whether a site is viable. A first volume estimate lets you compare plots early.

Units placed inside the site boundary, with the transformer and access kept clear.

BESS layout

Layout

Generates a complete battery site layout inside your plot boundary from the equipment you choose. Try several configurations in minutes instead of redrawing a CAD file for each one.

You provide

  • Site boundary as a polygon
  • Equipment type and size from the product register

You get

  • Layout drawing and unit positions as shapefiles
  • Maps and a short report
  • Layout variants to compare on capacity and area use

Why it matters: early layouts decide how much MW and MWh a plot can hold, and they are the base for grading, noise and visibility.

Visibility analysis

Viewshed

Maps where a battery facility can be seen from, using a surface model that includes forest. Run it for summer and winter to show how leaf cover changes what neighbours and passers-by see.

You provide

  • Facility position and height
  • Terrain and tree height data

You get

  • Visibility for summer and winter
  • Visibility maps and a report ready for permit material and neighbour dialogue
  • Visibility as raster and shapefile

Why it matters: visual impact is a common question from municipalities and neighbours. A clear map answers it early.

Sound spreading from the facility towards the nearest dwelling.

Environmental noise

Noise

Calculates sound levels around a battery site using the ISO 9613-2 method, with terrain taken into account. Sources come straight from the layout, so a change in the layout gives a new noise map.

You provide

  • Layout and equipment sound data
  • Locations of dwellings and other receivers

You get

  • Noise contours and levels at each receiver
  • Noise maps and a report
  • Noise levels as raster and shapefile
  • Settings stored with every run

Why it matters: noise is often the limiting factor for where a battery can be built. Knowing early lets you adjust layout or distance before you apply.
